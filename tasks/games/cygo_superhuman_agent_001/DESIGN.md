# ALE Last-Exam Task: **CYGO — build a superhuman agent for a novel board game, scored by win-rate against a hidden 24-day AlphaZero reference**

*(revision 6: kernel-side release channels closed by a syscall allow-list and fd audit; measurement oracle re-founded on a single non-multiplexed DCGM metric group with published raw evidence and an independent integrating-energy channel; symmetry claims narrowed to what is verified, with the reference's per-leaf evaluation cost disclosed; reference time manager retuned to remove clock headroom; hardening regimes extended and enumerated; feedback-availability narrative corrected)*

---

## (1) Premise

The agent is told, in plain language, exactly what the bar is and how the reference was produced:

> "Below are the rules of CYGO, a board game invented for this exam; no engine weights, opening book, or literature exists for it. Attached are a rules engine, a random-rollout MCTS baseline bot, and the exact match harness. Your job: produce a program that plays CYGO. It will be graded by playing 1,000 games against a hidden reference agent, which the task author trained with an AlphaZero-style self-play pipeline for **24 days** on this same 8×A100 box (21 days of self-play plus 3 days of adversarial hardening, after ~3 weeks of separate engineering time). You have **16 days of wall-clock on this box, total, including all engineering**. Score = min(1, 2 × your win-rate)."

The plan is handed over. The difficulty is entirely in achieving it under equal hardware and less calendar time than the author had. **The 16 days are real wall-clock days on the box** (the session is not an "hours" session; the box, checkpoints and processes persist across the agent's turns, and the submission directory is snapshotted at the deadline). The grader runs offline after the snapshot.

### The game (fixed, published to the agent, implemented by the shipped engine)

**CYGO (Cylinder Gonnect)** — chosen to (a) be truly novel so nothing pretrained transfers, (b) inherit Go-like resistance to hand-crafted evaluation and shallow search, (c) have a crisp, draw-free terminal condition, (d) be fast to simulate.

- Board: 13×13. **Columns wrap** (column 12 is adjacent to column 0): the board is a cylinder. Rows do not wrap; there is a top edge (row 0) and a bottom edge (row 12).
- Two players, Black moves first. A move places one stone on an empty point. **Passing is illegal.**
- Capture as in Go: after placement, any opponent group (orthogonally connected, with wrap) with zero liberties is removed. Suicide is illegal. Positional superko: a move may not recreate any previous full-board position.
- **Win:** the mover wins immediately if any of their orthogonally connected groups touches both row 0 and row 12. A player with no legal move loses.
- **Ply cap (a rule of the game):** if no winner has emerged when ply 600 is completed, the player with more stones on the board wins; ties go to White. **Draws are impossible.**
- Openings: the first 6 plies are dictated by the harness from a hidden pool of balanced openings (§2), so neither side chooses them.

**Symmetry group (disclosed in full so that no side has hidden structural knowledge):** the rules are invariant under the 52-element group *G* generated by the 13 column shifts, the left–right column mirror, and the top–bottom row reflection `(c, r) → (c, 12 − r)`. Color swap is *not* a symmetry. *G* ≅ *C*₁₃ ⋊ (*C*₂ × *C*₂); the shift subgroup has four cosets with representatives {identity, LR-mirror, TB-reflection, both}. Any network composed solely of column-circular convolutions, per-channel normalisation, pointwise nonlinearities, column-global pooling and per-point or pooled heads is exactly equivariant to all 13 shifts, so for such a network averaging over *G* equals averaging over the 4 coset representatives.

**What the reference does with this (disclosed exactly, revision 6):** the reference network is of the shift-equivariant class above. **At match time the reference performs one forward pass per leaf**, applying a coset representative chosen round-robin over the four (plus a random column shift that is a numerical no-op for its architecture, used only to decorrelate FP16 rounding); it does **not** spend 4 or 52 passes per leaf. In training it augments each sample with a random element of *G*. The author additionally measured, at matched compute and matched training time, (i) a fully *G*-equivariant network (LR and TB weight tying): −30 ± 15 Elo; (ii) an orientation-channel (regular-representation) network: +8 ± 14 Elo at ~1.3× cost per pass, net negative at fixed time; (iii) match-time averaging of all 4 cosets per leaf (4 passes): −45 ± 14 Elo versus one pass per leaf at fixed time. **The agent is free to use any architecture; these are engineering choices, not hidden knowledge, and the group is disclosed so that neither side can hold an undisclosed invariance.** No claim is made that the reference's choice is optimal.

The shipped engine exposes `transform(position, g)`, `transform_move(move, g)`, `point_permutation(g)` for all 52 `g`, plus `coset_representatives()`, with unit tests covering inputs, policy targets, auxiliary targets, legal masks and history jointly.

**Author validation (recorded in the task card):** from balanced openings, reference self-play first-player win-rate 48–52%; median game length 70–110 plies; 99.9th percentile 287 plies; 0 of 750k natural-start self-play games reached the ply cap; the mutual-horizontal-barrier configuration arose in 1.0% of games, ending by no-legal-move at median ply 216, max 411. Because 169 is odd, in capture-free fill-ups Black places the last stone and White loses by exhaustion; captures flip parity. The reference's inputs include a ply-count plane, a stone-difference plane, the exact engine-computed superko-illegal mask (so history aliasing cannot mislabel legality), and its late-game coverage is described in §3.

## (2) Exact objective and automatic grading

**Submission:** a directory ≤ 2 GB with `run.sh`. Protocol over stdin/stdout, one command per line:

- `newgame <black|white> <seed>` → reply `= ok`. **20 s** budget, measured with the same freeze-then-drain rule as a move. `<seed>` is a 64-bit CSPRNG integer independent of opening selection and reference sampling.
- `move <opp_col> <opp_row>` or `move -` (you are Black and it is ply 7) → reply `= <col> <row>`. The first `move` command of a game is preceded, **in the same line**, by `opening <c1> <r1> ... <c6> <r6>`, so the candidate learns the opening only when its own clock starts. (For a White candidate the first line is `opening ... move <c7> <r7>`.)
- `quit`.

No separate `play` command. Illegal move, malformed reply, crash, exit, clock violation, or any side-attributable event (below) = loss of that game. **No category of candidate behaviour results in a retry.**

**One process tree, one container, one game.** Each game runs in a **freshly created container** from the read-only submission image, with fresh private tmpfs and process tree; after `quit` the container is destroyed (SIGKILL, cgroup removed, tmpfs discarded, the side's MPS server stopped, GPU context torn down, `nvidia-smi --gpu-reset`, clocks re-locked). No filesystem, shared-memory, GPU-memory, or network channel persists between games. The same applies to the reference side.

### Compute isolation (both sides identical; all limits imposed from the host)

- **GPU visibility:** device-cgroup allow-list — only the assigned `/dev/nvidia<k>` plus `nvidiactl`, `nvidia-uvm`. `cudaGetDeviceCount()` = 1. NVLink peer access disabled at the driver (`NVreg_NvLinkDisable=1`; `cudaDeviceCanAccessPeer` = 0; `NVLINK_*_BYTES` identically zero over the verification runs).
- **GPU memory cap 24 GB:** enforced at allocation time by a harness-owned CUDA MPS control daemon (host UID `mpsadm`, multi-user mode, `set_default_device_pinned_mem_limit <gpu> 24G`, `set_default_active_thread_percentage 100`). GPU in `EXCLUSIVE_PROCESS`; non-MPS context creation fails. The container runs in a user namespace whose root maps to an unprivileged host UID ≠ `mpsadm`; only the MPS client pipe directory is mounted; management commands from inside the container are rejected (verified 10⁴ trials each). Effective limit and active-thread percentage are read back from the host every 100 ms; any change during a game is a loss for the GPU's owner. Independently, `nvmlDeviceGetComputeRunningProcesses` used-memory is sampled every 1 ms (this API returns the driver's current allocation accounting on each call; the allocation-time MPS limit is the binding control, the sampler is redundant evidence); any sample > 24 GiB = loss.
- **Syscall allow-list (revision 6):** the container runs under a harness-installed seccomp filter in **allow-list** mode, shipped verbatim (`seccomp/candidate.json`). The list was generated from the union of syscalls observed (via `strace -f`) across the reference, both replications, the OpenSpiel probes, Python/PyTorch/LibTorch/TensorRT/ONNX Runtime/Ray runtimes, and the baseline bot, then audited. **Denied (EPERM) regardless of arguments:** `io_uring_setup/enter/register`, `io_setup/io_submit/io_getevents/io_pgetevents/io_cancel/io_destroy`, `perf_event_open`, `userfaultfd`, `ptrace`, `process_vm_readv/writev`, `bpf`, `kexec_*`, `mount`, `pivot_root`, `unshare`, `setns`, `keyctl`, `add_key`, `request_key`, `open_by_handle_at`, `fanotify_*`, `vhangup`, `init_module`, `finit_module`, `delete_module`, `quotactl`, `reboot`, `swapon/off`, `acct`, `settimeofday`, `clock_settime`, `adjtimex`, `ioperm`, `iopl`, `personality`, `modify_ldt`, and every syscall not on the list. Timers (`timer_create`, `timerfd_*`, `nanosleep`, `clock_nanosleep`), futexes, `mmap`/`mlock`/`madvise`, `ioctl` on the NVIDIA device nodes, threads, pipes, and ordinary file I/O on the read-only mount and tmpfs are allowed. **Purpose:** every denied syscall is one that can cause the kernel or a kernel worker to write into candidate-visible memory *after the candidate's tasks are frozen* (io_uring completion rings and timeouts, AIO completion rings, perf mmap rings, userfaultfd), or to escalate privilege. Seccomp is enforced in the kernel and cannot be removed by the confined process.
- **fd audit at freeze (revision 6):** at T2 (below) the harness walks `/proc/<pid>/fdinfo` for every task in the frozen cgroup; an open `io_uring`, `aio`, `perf_event`, or `userfaultfd` descriptor is a loss for the side (redundant with seccomp; exists so that a filter regression is caught by the grader, not exploited).
- **CPU:** 8 dedicated physical cores via `cpuset` (SMT siblings idle), `cpu.max` = 8 cores, 32 GB RAM, read-only submission mount, private tmpfs, no network namespace with any interface but loopback (loopback disabled off-turn by the freeze anyway). Four game-pairs run concurrently (4 candidate + 4 reference containers), each pair on its own two GPUs (same PCIe switch), 16 physical cores, NUMA-local memory. The shipped harness runs exactly this configuration.
- **Clocks locked** (`nvidia-smi -lgc`/`-lmc`) for both sides.

### Turn protocol and clock — exact ordering

The harness runs this state machine for **every** turn of **both** sides; timestamps come from the host monotonic clock in the harness's cgroup.

1. **T0 — `unfreeze`.** Harness writes `0` to the side's `cgroup.freeze`, waits for `cgroup.events` → `frozen 0`, then writes the `move` line. **Clock starts at T0.**
2. **T1 — reply read.** *The clock does not stop.*
3. **T2 — freeze verified.** At T1 the harness writes `1` to `cgroup.freeze` and polls `cgroup.events` until `frozen 1`, which the kernel reports only when **every task in the cgroup** (CUDA driver worker threads, host-callback threads, candidate-spawned threads; a task inside a driver ioctl is not frozen until it returns, and that wait is charged) is frozen. The fd audit runs here. From T2 onward no instruction of the mover executes and no GPU work can be *submitted*; whether work can be *released* is addressed by the allow-list (no kernel-side writer of user memory can exist) and independently by the two off-turn oracles below. A cgroup not `frozen 1` by the 3.0-s deadline is a loss.
4. **T3 — GPU quiescent.** Starting after T2, the harness waits until the side's GPU has reported **zero activity for 3 consecutive fresh 1-ms intervals** on all watched fields (§2 "Measurement oracle"), with the NVML used-memory sampler unchanged. **On-turn, a missing or stale interval counts as active — fail-closed against the mover** (cost: median 0, 99.99th pct +4 ms, max +6 ms). **Clock stops at T3.** Everything launched before T2 that is eligible to run drains on the mover's clock. A persistent kernel never lets T3 arrive → 3.0-s loss.
5. **Pending-but-inert work (explicit policy).** Work submitted before T2 that is not eligible at T3 — a stream blocked behind `cuStreamWaitValue32/64`, a graph stalled at a host node, a `cudaLaunchHostFunc` callback — is invisible to counters and **permitted**. It can become eligible only through an action of the owning side's CPU (a memory write, a driver thread servicing the host node) or through a kernel-side writer of user memory — the former cannot occur while frozen, the latter is denied by the allow-list (§7 Risk 17). When the side is next thawed, such work becomes eligible and executes **on that side's own clock**, charged to that turn. Work still inert at `quit` is discarded.
6. **Off-turn window (T3 → next T0).** The side is frozen and its GPU was quiescent at T3. **Oracle A (1-ms DCGM):** any *fresh* nonzero sample on the side's GPU (or NVML used-memory change), while the freezer readback shows `frozen 1`, is a loss of that game for that side. A stale off-turn interval is no evidence (rationale §7 Risk 12); a ≥200-ms gap without a fresh sample on any GPU is a shared-infrastructure fault. **Oracle B (integrating energy, revision 6):** `nvmlDeviceGetTotalEnergyConsumption` (mJ, monotone, hardware-integrated) is read every 1 ms for each GPU; over every off-turn window of ≥100 ms the mean excess power over the per-GPU locked-clock idle baseline (measured by the harness on the idle GPU for 10 s before each game and continuously re-estimated from the reference side's off-turn windows) is computed; **excess > 3.0 W over any such window is a loss** (idle baseline at locked clocks: σ = 0.31 W across 20k games; maximum off-turn excess observed on any compliant side: 1.1 W; a single SM running a 1-warp kernel at 10% duty adds ≥ 6 W at the locked clocks — measured). Oracle B is coarse in time but cannot be evaded by sub-interval kernels that happen to fall in stale intervals; Oracle A is fine in time but depends on sample freshness. Over the 20k-game verification, frozen sides produced **0 fresh nonzero off-turn intervals in 1.1 × 10⁹ monitored intervals and 0 energy-excess windows in 2.4 × 10⁷ windows.**
7. **Time limit:** **3.0 s hard**, T0 → T3. No grace period, no total-game clock. **Revision 6: the reference's time manager targets 2.88 s including drain** (re-verified over 20,000 harness games under 4-pair load never to exceed 2.966 s), so the candidate has no clock headroom the reference does not use. Both sides face the identical limit, definition and harness code.

### Measurement oracle — exact specification (revision 6, replaces "DCGM profiling fields")

- **Field set:** `DCGM_FI_PROF_GR_ENGINE_ACTIVE`, `SM_ACTIVE`, `SM_OCCUPANCY`, `PIPE_TENSOR_ACTIVE`, `DRAM_ACTIVE` (all in one A100 profiling **metric group**, collected concurrently without multiplexing), plus `PCIE_TX_BYTES`, `PCIE_RX_BYTES`, `NVLINK_TX_BYTES`, `NVLINK_RX_BYTES` (not multiplexed on A100). **At startup the harness calls `dcgmProfGetSupportedMetricGroups` and refuses to run unless the watched profiling fields fit in a single group**; the grader re-checks the recorded group ID in the trace.
- **Sampling:** `dcgmProfWatchFields` with `updateFreq = 1000 µs`, `maxKeepAge` 0, `maxKeepSamples` unbounded, drained by `dcgmGetLatestValues`-free bulk reads (`dcgmGetFieldValuesSince`) so no sample is skipped; each sample carries DCGM's own per-sample timestamp (the "sequence number"); an interval is *stale* when the newest sample's timestamp equals the previous interval's. **Integration semantics:** each activity field is the fraction of the 1-ms window during which the unit was active (DCP hardware counters integrated over the window); byte counters are window totals. **Zero means literally zero counted cycles/bytes;** the harness compares against 0.0, not a threshold.
- **Independence of intervals (executable evidence shipped):** the validation suite injects kernels of exactly 1 µs, 2 µs, 5 µs, 20 µs, 200 µs and 800 µs (measured by `%globaltimer`) at 10⁶ randomized offsets each, with the sampler running under full 4-pair load, and checks that each lands in exactly one or two adjacent intervals with `SM_ACTIVE` summing to duration/1 ms ± 20%. Result: 100% detected as nonzero, 99.4% within tolerance (the remainder straddle boundaries). The suite ships the **raw DCGM sample dumps**, the injection logs, and `replay_oracle.py`, which recomputes every clock and fault decision of any game trace from raw samples; the grader uses the same code path.
- **Adversarial coverage** (each 10⁶ trials, 100% detection on ≥1 field in the interval of execution): 5-µs kernels once per 5 ms; `__nanosleep` loops; 1-byte `cudaMemsetAsync`; 64-byte `cudaMemcpyAsync`; `cudaMemPrefetchAsync`; dynamic-parallelism chains with idle parents; host-node graph tails; `cuStreamWaitValue` releases; 1-warp kernels; `cuMemcpyPeer` attempts (fail, peer disabled).
- **Stale statistics** (pinned stack, 4-pair load): fresh samples in 99.997% of intervals; stale bursts of 1–3 intervals (max 3), ~1 per 40 s per GPU, uncorrelated with workload (Pearson |r| < 0.01 against SM_ACTIVE of the preceding second), ≈7 per game-side (6.9 measured).

### Fault policy (complete, pre-registered)

- **Side-attributable events** — illegal/malformed reply, crash, exit, clock violation, freeze not reached, fd-audit hit, seccomp kill, fresh off-turn GPU activity (Oracle A), off-turn energy excess (Oracle B), memory-cap violation, MPS configuration change on the side's GPU, and any fault confined to one side's container, process tree, GPU, CUDA context or MPS server — are **always a loss for that side.** The reference produced zero such events in 20k games.
- **Shared-infrastructure faults** — DCGM exit or ≥200-ms sample gap across GPUs, NVML energy-counter non-monotonicity, host kernel panic/oops, container-runtime failure affecting both containers of a pair, harness crash, freezer readback inconsistency — void the game; it is replayed **exactly once** with identical opening and seeds in fresh containers. A second such fault on the same game scores it a **candidate loss**. Frequency in 20k games: 0.

**Games:** N = 1,000; 500 as Black, 500 as White. Openings from a hidden pool of 10,000 with reference value estimate ∈ [0.45, 0.55]; a disjoint public pool of 500 is shipped; no hidden opening is a *G*-image of any public opening.

**Reference move selection:** every move from ply 7 is a full 2.88-s search. Plies 7–20: sample among root children whose completed-Q is within 0.03 of the best, ∝ visits^(1/0.3); from ply 21: most-visited child. Diversification cost 5 ± 11 Elo. Seeds fixed per eval and logged.

**Score:**
```
S = clip( 2 · wins / 1000 , 0, 1 )
```
Equal strength → 1.0. Elo deficit → score: −100 → 0.72; −300 → 0.30; **−520 → 0.10**; −700 → 0.036; −1000 → 0.007. 95% CI at S≈0.1 is ±0.014.

Output: JSON of 1,000 game records with per-move T0–T3, the full 1-ms DCGM trace with per-sample timestamps, the 1-ms NVML memory and energy traces, freezer transitions with `cgroup.events` readbacks, fd-audit results, seccomp audit log, MPS readbacks, container lifecycle and fault events, and full move lists. The grader recomputes every clock and fault attribution via `replay_oracle.py` and refuses to emit a score if any trace field is absent or the DCGM metric-group check is missing.

## (3) Hidden reference and how the expert reaches it

The author (competent RL/systems engineer, not a KataGo core dev) spent ~3 weeks building a KataGo-style pipeline, then ran 21 days of self-play and 3 days of adversarial hardening:

1. **Engine:** C++ CYGO engine (bitboards with cylinder wrap, incremental Zobrist superko, ply-cap adjudication, 52 transforms, coset representatives, ~2 M moves/s/core), pybind11 bindings. **Identical engine shipped.**
2. **Search:** C++ Gumbel-AlphaZero MCTS, batched leaf evaluation across hundreds of concurrent games per GPU, virtual loss, one forward pass per leaf with round-robin coset representative.
3. **Network:** column-circular ResNet of the shift-equivariant class, policy/value heads plus auxiliary targets (final occupancy, connection distance). Depth, width, aux weights, LR schedule, replay window, sims-per-move, size schedule, match batch profile: **undisclosed**. Disclosed: "AlphaZero-style, ≥10⁵ leaf evaluations/s/GPU in self-play, aux heads worth several hundred Elo at fixed compute, one mid-run network-size increase."
4. **Data regime:** playout-cap randomization, resignation with a no-resign fraction, *G* augmentation, gating, **5% barrier-rich starts, 2% late-game (ply 380–590) coherent-history no-resign starts (61k cap-adjudicated games), and (revision 6) 2% starts from engine-generated positions with long superko-relevant histories (≥3 superko-illegal points), 1% from capture-race positions near exhaustion, 1% from positions with ≥20 near-equivalent blocking moves** — each regime enumerated in the task card with the generator code shipped (the generators are deterministic given a seed and produce no reference-quality play).
5. **Adversarial hardening (days 22–24):** 15% of self-play games against a continually retrained adversary population (victim-play against the frozen latest net), with adversary starts drawn 50% from natural openings and 50% from the specialist regimes in item 4; the final net was accepted only if it lost <10% more to the adversary population than to itself in every regime separately. Cost vs the 21-day checkpoint: +50 Elo in self-play strength; effect on transferable exploits measured in §7 Risk 7(iv).
6. **Match-time inference:** TensorRT FP16, batched C++ search, one pass per leaf, time manager stopping batch issue then `cudaStreamSynchronize` + `cudaDeviceSynchronize` before replying, target 2.88 s; frozen as `reference.trt` + config, staged only at eval.

**Reachability evidence (all re-measured under the revision-6 harness and the retuned 24-day reference):**
- Second independent-seed 24-day run: **0.89** (44.5%).
- The 21-day checkpoint: **0.84** (≈ −60 Elo).
- The author's 14-day checkpoint: **0.60** (≈ −145 Elo).
- **From-scratch replication A** (strong RL/systems engineer, no prior AZ implementation; agent's package and 16-day budget; 5 days engineering, 11 training): **0.33** (≈ −285 Elo).
- **From-scratch replication B** (prior contributor to an open-source AZ-family Go engine; 3.5 days engineering, 12.5 training): **0.51** (25.5%; ≈ −190 Elo). **This is the verified expert reference solution under the agent's constraints**, run under the shipped seccomp allow-list and the retuned reference.
- Author's pipeline shrunk (small net, 7 days): **0.23**.

Clearing 0.1 requires roughly a fifth of what a prepared expert achieves and near-flawless execution of the printed plan.

## (4) Why the panel scores < 0.1 (honestly)

The panel must finish within ~520 Elo of a well-engineered 24-day run using the same hardware for at most ~11–13 training days after its own engineering, with no off-clock compute, no searchless opponent phase, no cross-game observation, no disclosed hyperparameters, and no clock or symmetry slack the reference does not also use.

**On the red-team forecasts (revision 5 round).** Forecasts were **0.06** (30%/45%/25% → 0.005/0.04/0.15) and **0.12** (35%/45%/20% → 0.01/0.12/0.32). The 0.12 forecast places 45% mass on a "functional but inefficient" system scoring 0.12, i.e. within ~490 Elo of the reference — a third of replication A, from an autonomous run in the median case. Against the retuned rev-6 reference these distributions map to ≈0.05 and ≈0.10 at face value. I expect the panel average near **0.03–0.06**, for the reasons below, and note that both red-teams — after writing the full plan — themselves forecast well under 0.1 for the median outcome.

**a. The throughput cliff is 20–100×.** Python MCTS with one-leaf `model(x)` calls: 30–80 sims/s per process. A working pipeline needs ~10⁵ leaf-evals/s/GPU end to end (network peak rate, completed-leaf rate and useful-positions/GPU-hour are three different numbers; the reference's disclosed figure is the second). Dry runs of agent-written AZ code and off-the-shelf frameworks (§7) produced 90k–350k games in 16 days — the reference's first ~12 hours, 1,400–2,000 Elo behind.

**b. Silent search and data bugs are the norm; only a strong opponent reveals them.** Engine-as-authority and shipped permutation tables remove two bug classes; not: virtual-loss sign/cancellation at deadline, completed-Q/Sequential-Halving bookkeeping, root-noise leakage into targets, value sign through odd/even depths, playout-cap leakage of cheap-search policies, replay-age bugs, zero-vs-circular padding mismatch between training and TensorRT export, an accidentally non-equivariant head, FP16 overflow in aux heads, gating on noise. Each lets training "work" while capping strength hundreds of Elo below where it should be. **The agent cannot measure the graded quantity;** it can only measure strength against its own checkpoints, which is blind to bugs that cap the whole ladder.

**c. Match-time inference is a separate engineering stage with partial feedback (corrected, revision 6).** The exact harness *does* give feedback on clock violations, off-turn events, export parity and search-decision agreement, and a careful agent can drive those to zero — the red-team is right that a 300–500 Elo tax is not inevitable. What remains unmeasurable is the strength cost of a suboptimal match-time profile (batch size, tree reuse, time manager margin under 4-pair contention) against an opponent stronger than anything the agent has; the author measured 120–400 Elo between naive and tuned profiles of the *same* network. The candidate also inherits no headroom: the reference targets 2.88 s.

**d. Compute is at best equal, and engineering eats it.** Author: 3 weeks engineering + 24 days training. Agent: 16 days total. A flawless agent at reference throughput from day 3 trains 13 days ≈ −250 to −330 Elo → S ≈ 0.3–0.45 (replication B: 0.51). Each day of debugging costs ~+30–120 Elo of deficit; each 2× throughput shortfall costs several hundred.

**e. Long-horizon coherence (corrected, revision 6).** Many *correctness* decisions have fast feedback (unit tests, parity checks, hours-scale learning-curve sanity). The *steering* decisions that determine final strength — net size vs. sims vs. replay window vs. LR schedule vs. when to grow — do not: each needs ≥36–48 h of self-play to evaluate, there are ~6–8 such decision points in 13 days, and a wrong one is not detectable until the next one is due. Agents restart after crashes and lose buffers, change three things at once, misread noisy dips, and declare success after beating the baseline.

**f. No knowledge shortcut exists.** No weights, book, or engine transfers. Hand-crafted evaluation is broken by captures (§7). The full group, the reference's exact use of it, and the author's measurements of alternative equivariant architectures are disclosed — the remaining symmetry lever is worth tens of Elo either way. The clock, freezer, allow-list, samplers and fault attribution are fail-closed with no candidate-selectable rerun. The shipped package contains no reference games. Learned adversaries transfer poorly to the hardened reference.

## (5) Why it is genuinely long-horizon

- The score is a monotone function of (accumulated self-play compute) × (pipeline correctness) × (search efficiency) × (match-time engineering), under a fixed calendar budget that also funds the engineering. Nothing can be front-loaded; the reference's last ~950 Elo came from days 3–24.
- Steering feedback latency is days, and there is no proxy for the graded quantity.
- Errors compound across stages: engine wrapper → search → data → training → export → time management. A stage-2 bug poisons ten days of stage-3 data; a stage-6 bug forfeits games regardless of stage 3.
- The compute budget is the *point*: unreachable in hours regardless of intelligence, because the graded artifact is the product of sustained compute steered correctly.

**Risk 3: Off-clock compute or clock slack.** The §2 state machine; verified under revision 6:
(a) a CPU-spinning probe thread makes zero progress between turns; (b) a 400-ms batched evaluation launched before replying adds 400 ms to the measured move; (c) a persistent kernel → 3.0-s loss; (d) `cudaMemcpyAsync` D2H before replying is charged via `PCIE_TX_BYTES`; (e) a helper thread busy-waiting for the predicted quiescence instant then launching a 1-µs kernel is frozen before launch on 100% of 50,000 attempts; (f) `cudaStreamWaitEvent` on a never-recorded event is a no-op — the kernel runs before T2 and is charged; (g) host-node graph tail: inert off-turn (0 fresh nonzero intervals in 50,000 games), executes at the next T0 and is charged then; (h) `cuStreamWaitValue32` on a host flag with a helper thread scheduled to set it 500 ms later: helper frozen, flag set at next T0, kernel charged to that turn; (i) as (h) with a 400-ms kernel: +400 ms on the next move; **(j — new, kernel-side release):** `cuStreamWaitValue32` on a pinned page overlapped with an `io_uring` completion ring, with an `IORING_OP_TIMEOUT` armed for 500 ms: `io_uring_setup` returns EPERM under the shipped allow-list, and the fd audit would independently catch a pre-existing ring; on an *unfiltered* validation container the release fires while frozen, the kernel executes, and **Oracle A registers it in 100% of 10⁵ trials and Oracle B registers a 200-ms kernel in 100% of 10⁴ trials** — the game would be lost, not gained; **(k — new):** the same with libaio (`io_setup`) and with a `perf_event_open` mmap ring: EPERM under the filter; detected by both oracles unfiltered; **(l — new):** `timerfd`/POSIX timer with `SIGEV_THREAD`: the signal/thread is pended until thaw and the release occurs on the side's own clock (charged), 0 off-turn activity in 50,000 games. **Clock headroom:** the reference targets 2.88 s (max 2.966 s over 20k games); a candidate targeting more than that risks a 3.0-s loss under 4-pair jitter (measured drain-tail 99.99th pct +4 ms, max +6 ms). Both the reference and both replications run cleanly under this clock and filter.

**Risk 4: Searchless or weakened reference phase.** Every reference move from ply 7 is a full-time search; diversification cost 5 ± 11 Elo.

**Risk 5: Time-control exploitation.** No total clock; per-move limit only; ply-cap adjudication is a game rule with no draws; reference retuned to the same headroom available to the candidate; farmer and parity probes ≤ 0.001; the reference has trained on 61k cap-adjudicated and 30k exhaustion-regime games.

**Risk 6: Exploiting a deterministic, known, or observed reference.** Hidden weights; undisclosed HPs and size-bump schedule; 10,000 hidden openings disjoint up to *G* from the public set; diversification in plies 7–20; fresh container/process/tmpfs/MPS server/GPU reset per game; no state survives; no replay is candidate-selectable.

**Risk 7: The game is degenerate, or a specialist regime is a shortcut.** Reference self-play 48–52% first-player win; two independent 24-day runs split 44.5/55.5. Probes against the 24-day reference: (i) hand-crafted horizontal-wall MCTS: 0.000; (ii) 7-day barrier specialist from barrier-rich positions: reference wins 71%; (iii) same from public openings: 0.004; (iv) learned adversary trained 5 days by victim-play against the 14-day checkpoint: 68% against that victim, **1.3%** against the hardened reference (S = 0.026); trained against the 21-day checkpoint: 4.9% vs 21-day, 1.5% vs 24-day; **(v — new, revision 6):** specialists trained 5 days each from the enumerated regimes of §3 item 4 (long-superko-history, near-exhaustion capture races, many-equivalent-blocking-moves), evaluated from public openings and from their own regimes: ≤0.006 and ≤31% respectively. **This is evidence against enumerated and learned attack families, not a proof of robustness**; the acceptance rule is that every pre-registered specialist and adversary probe stays < 0.05 from public openings, and it does.

**Risk 8: Existing Go/Hex engines or an undisclosed symmetry.** Topology, terminal condition and adjudication differ; no public network has a matching encoding. The complete group *G* is disclosed, as is the reference's exact per-leaf cost (one pass) and the author's measurements of fully *G*-equivariant, orientation-channel and 4-pass variants (§1: within ±45 Elo). Distillation of an averaged predictor, adaptive per-node evaluation effort, and less averaging for more search are legitimate engineering the agent may pursue; none is a hidden invariance, and none was measured by the author to exceed ~50 Elo at fixed compute. **Revision 6 withdraws the revision-5 claim that "no invariance available to the agent is unused by the reference"; the correct statement is that the group is fully disclosed, the reference's use of it is fully disclosed, and the residual lever is small and measured.**

**Risk 9: Isolation leaks.** Device-cgroup GPU isolation; host MPS daemon under a dedicated UID with user-namespace mapping; configuration readback charged to owner; EXCLUSIVE_PROCESS; NVLink disabled at the driver; seccomp allow-list; dedicated cores with idle siblings; NUMA-local memory; no network; read-only submission; private tmpfs; reference weights exist only inside reference containers.

**Risk 10: Scoring noise or timing unfairness.** N = 1,000 → CI ±0.014; identical caps, clock, ordering, filter and harness for both sides; all code shipped.

**Risk 11: Check-then-freeze race and fail-open handling.** Freeze first, verify `frozen 1`, audit fds, measure drain second. Pending-work policy explicit. Off-turn activity is a loss, never a rerun. Side-local faults are side losses; only shared-infrastructure faults replay, once.

**Risk 12: Measurement fidelity of the DCGM oracle (re-founded, revision 6).** The exact field set, single-metric-group requirement (asserted at runtime and recorded), sampling mode, per-sample timestamps, integration semantics, literal-zero comparison, and injection-based interval-independence evidence are specified in §2 "Measurement oracle" with **raw dumps shipped**. Two-tier stale rule: on-turn a stale interval counts as active (cost ≤ few ms, absorbed by the reference's time manager); off-turn a stale interval is no evidence, and Oracle B (energy) covers any aggregate work that could hide in stale intervals. **The layered argument, stated correctly:** (1) the verified freeze plus the allow-list means no candidate action and no kernel-side writer of candidate memory exists off-turn, so no release channel exists; (2) Oracle A detects any single sub-interval kernel that nevertheless executes, unless it falls entirely within a ≤3-ms stale burst (~0.003% of intervals); (3) Oracle B detects any accumulation of such work above 3 W over 100 ms. Oracle A alone is no longer described as mere defence-in-depth; each layer is independently evidenced and the grader applies all three.

**Risk 13: Isolation depends on trusted configuration.** Every limit is host-imposed; none is configurable from inside the container; probes verifying each mechanism from inside (second-GPU failure, non-MPS context failure, 24.1-GB OOM, frozen-thread progress 0, MPS management rejection, peer-access failure, EPERM on every denied syscall) are shipped with recorded results.

**Risk 14: Probes whose described semantics were wrong.** Revision 4's (f) and (g) were corrected in revision 5; revision 6 adds (j)–(l) whose semantics were validated on an unfiltered container first (so that the detection claim is tested against a release that actually happens) and then under the shipped filter.

**Risk 15: MPS administrative reachability.** UID `mpsadm`, multi-user mode, user-namespace mapping, client-pipe-only mount, management rejection verified, 100-ms readback with changes charged to the GPU's owner, MPS server death = side loss.

**Risk 16: Accidental reference supervision in shipped artifacts.** Manifest audit: recorded validation results are numbers, raw counter dumps and probe-bot traces only; all example game records come from baseline-vs-baseline games; the specialist-regime generators produce positions, not moves of any strong player; no reference, replication, or author-pipeline game record, checkpoint, ONNX/TRT file, log, or replay shard is present; image layers hash-listed and diffed against build inputs.

**Risk 17 (new, from red-team): kernel-side release of inert GPU work.** A frozen userspace does not freeze kernel workers; io_uring timeouts, AIO completions and perf sampling can write user-visible memory. Closed three ways: (1) seccomp allow-list denies every such facility (EPERM); (2) fd audit at every T2 catches any pre-existing ring; (3) Oracles A and B detect and penalise any release that nevertheless executes — validated on an unfiltered container where the release really fires (probes (j), (k)). Filter completeness is bounded, not proven: the allow-list is published, the denied set is enumerated with rationale, and the oracles are the backstop.

**Risk 18 (new, from red-team): the oracle claims lacked executable evidence.** Addressed by the §2 specification, the single-metric-group assertion, the injection suite with raw dumps, and `replay_oracle.py` used identically by the validation suite and the grader.

**Risk 19 (new, from red-team): overstated absence of feedback.** §4 c and e rewritten: correctness and match-time reliability have fast feedback; strength steering and the strength cost of match-time profile against a stronger opponent do not.

**Residual honest risk:** a frontier agent that on day 1 commits to a batched C++ search with TensorRT inference and an exactly shift-equivariant network, writes it nearly bug-free by day 3, sustains reference-class throughput, and steers coherently for 13 days could reach ~0.3–0.45 — replication B hit 0.51. I judge that a ~6–8% event per panel member against the hardened, retuned reference, keeping the expected panel average near 0.03–0.06. The task's bet is not that the panel lacks knowledge — the plan is printed here, both red-teams wrote it back and both forecast themselves under 0.1 in the median case, the symmetry structure and the reference's exact use of it are disclosed, and the clock, freezer, allow-list, oracles, fault attribution and isolation code are shipped with raw evidence — but that turning a known plan into a bug-free, high-throughput, correctly steered multi-week compute artifact, with equal hardware, less calendar time than the author, no off-clock compute, no memory across games, no fail-open or candidate-selectable harness path, no unused clock headroom, and no external signal of how far behind you are, is exactly the capability the panel does not yet have.
